# Product-cell sibling cgroups: read-only ARM inventory

The pinned Python `product_cell_supervisor.py` starts each product cell with `systemd-run --unit=hol-product-cell-<32 hex>.service` and observes it at `/sys/fs/cgroup/system.slice/<unit>`. The Rust contained-interpreter probe also creates its cgroup directly beneath `system.slice`. These are siblings: killing a future Rust controller cgroup alone would not prove that a product cell stopped. The Linux worker must bind every product-cell unit created during a job to that job, terminate it on timeout or interruption, and confirm the unit and its process tree are absent before writing a terminal cleanup success. Moving cells into a job-owned slice is an alternative only after fresh Python/Rust and systemd evidence.

`gauntlet-worker-state::observe_product_cell_units` is a Root-only, read-only point-in-time inventory of exact `hol-product-cell-<32 lowercase hex>.service` directories. It opens `system.slice` and each matching unit without following the final symlink, checks ownership, write permissions and directory identity, caps the inventory at 256 units, and refuses malformed lookalikes or symlinks. `require_no_product_cell_units` treats any existing unit as an admission blocker. Neither call owns a systemd lease or prevents a unit appearing after the scan; no worker dispatch or cleanup is authorized by this proof.
